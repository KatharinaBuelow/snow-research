    Ensemble Assessment & Statistical Robustness

    To statistically assess whether temporal changes in a climate variable have a systematic cause or can be explained by short-term random fluctuations, the statistical significance of climate change was calculated for each variable at every individual grid point in each simulation.

    A climate change is defined as significant if, according to a Mann–Whitney U test, it can be explained by random fluctuations with a probability of less than 5%.

    For year-to-year variations in temperature and precipitation, a Brown–Forsythe test is used instead.

    Robustness Categories

    Category Criterion
    Robust Increase ≥ 2/3 of simulations show an increase and ≥ 50% show a significant increase
    Robust Decrease ≥ 2/3 of simulations show a decrease and ≥ 50% show a significant decrease
    Tendency toward Increase ≥ 2/3 of simulations show an increase, but < 50% show a significant increase
    Tendency toward Decrease ≥ 2/3 of simulations show a decrease, but < 50% show a significant decrease
    Unclear No 2/3 majority on direction of change, but ≥ 50% show significant changes
    No Change No 2/3 majority on direction of change and < 50% show significant changes

    Significance and robust change

    Statistical significance of climate-change signals is assessed for each climate variable at each individual grid point and for each simulation.

    • A change is defined as significant if it cannot be explained by random (natural) fluctuations with a probability of less than 5% (\(p < 0.05\)) according to a Mann–Whitney U test.

    A scenario ensemble is classified as showing a robust increase if:

    • at least 2/3 of the simulations show an increase, and
    • at least 50% of the simulations show a significant increase.

    Analogously, a robust decrease is defined if:

    • at least 2/3 of the simulations show a decrease, and
    • at least 50% of the simulations show a significant decrease.
